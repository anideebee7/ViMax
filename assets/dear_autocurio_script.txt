🗝 Wonder Gate 3 — Q&A: "Why Is the Sky Different Colors at Sunset?"

⚡ Static Station — Q&A: "What Is Static Electricity? Why Do Socks Stick & Hair Stand Up?"

[Scene: A plaza carpeted in shag rugs. Giant balloons drift lazily. A lightning-bolt arch crackles with friendly zaps.]

Kid Voice (VO):
"Dear Autocurio, why does my hair stand up when I rub a balloon? Why do socks stick from the dryer?"

Autocurio (rubbing a balloon on his arm panel; hair on a wig pops up like porcupine quills):
"Meet static electricity—electric charges that build up and don't flow away right away."

DotDot (projects a kid-friendly electron and proton graphic):
"Everything's made of atoms with positive protons and negative electrons. When certain materials rub, electrons transfer—one object loses a few, becomes positively charged; the other gains, becomes negatively charged. Opposite charges attract, like tiny magnets; like charges repel."

Tinker (reveals "The Static Sleuth," a stand with hanging bits of tissue paper and a dial):
"Charge this balloon… now bring it close—"
[Tissues leap up and stick.]
"Ta-da! The balloon gave the tissues a little charge and attracted them."

DotDot:
"Dry air helps static build up. Humid air lets charges leak away. In dryers, different fabrics rub together, charging and sticking. Dryer sheets add molecules that help charges spread out."

Gloop (hair—well, jelly—spiking adorably):
"I am a thunderjelly!"

Whispurr (touches a doorknob; a tiny spark goes tink. She blinks, unimpressed.)

Autocurio (to camera):
"Static = charge imbalance. Rub → electrons move → sticky surprises. Zap safely: touch something metal first, or use a humidifier."
